Setting type for a long read

A field guide to serif text on screens: measure, rhythm, and the small marks that carry the voice of a page.

Published 1 November 2026 · 7 min read · Źródło: the editors of Quiet Type

Measure and rhythm

Good body text is rarely noticed. Readers settle into a rhythm of about sixty-six characters per line, and the typographer’s job is to keep that rhythm unbroken: consistent colour, quiet punctuation, and numerals that sit comfortably among the words. In 2024 the average article was read on a phone, at 390 pixels wide, in short bursts — so everything from the extraordinarily long compound words of the German “Donaudampfschifffahrtsgesellschaft” to the unassuming ellipsis… has to hold up.

Accents matter, too. Café menus, Zürich street signs, the Polish city of Łódź, the Spanish piñata, a Danish smørrebrød, Straße in Hamburg, the French œuvre and naïve façade: each is a small test of whether a subset kept its Latin Extended-A characters. Italic text should look right as well: the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog, and the bold inside italics matters, as does Łukasz’s name in italic.

Symbols outside the core set: a licence costs €20 per year, and the trademark sign looks like this™ in running text. Prices are not tax advice.
